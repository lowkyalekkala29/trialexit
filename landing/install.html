Install the TrialExit extension

Two minutes, six steps. The extension spots a free trial the moment you start it, keeps an eye on your subscriptions, and warns you before you're charged, so you don't lose money to something you forgot.

Early access — this is temporary. Our extension is in review on the Chrome Web Store. Until it's approved, early users install it manually with the steps below. When the store version is live we'll email you a one-click link. You won't lose anything: your trials are saved in your account.
⬇ Download TrialExit for Chrome Version {{VERSION}} · about 100 KB · works in Chrome, Edge and Brave on a computer (not on phones)

  1. Unzip the file you downloaded

    Find trialexit-extension.zip in your Downloads folder.

    WindowsRight-click the file → Extract All… → Extract.
    MacDouble-click the file. A folder appears next to it.

    Move the new trialexit-extension folder somewhere you won't delete it, like Documents. Chrome runs the extension from this folder, so keep it.

  2. Open Chrome's extensions page

    Copy this address, paste it into Chrome's address bar and press Enter:

    chrome://extensions

    Using Edge? Use edge://extensions. Using Brave? Use brave://extensions.

  3. Turn on “Developer mode”

    Top-right corner of that page. This just lets Chrome install extensions that aren't from the store yet.

    ExtensionsDeveloper mode

  4. Click “Load unpacked” and choose the folder

    A new row of buttons appears. Click Load unpacked, then select the trialexit-extension folder (the one you unzipped) and click Select / Open.

    Load unpackedPack extensionUpdate
    📁 Documents　📁 trialexit-extension　← choose this one　　📄 manifest.json

    TrialExit now appears in the list, and its welcome page opens. ✓ Installed.

  5. Pin it to your toolbar

    Click the puzzle-piece icon 🧩 next to the address bar, then the pin 📌 next to TrialExit. You'll see a red number on the icon whenever a trial is about to charge.

  6. Sign in

    In the TrialExit welcome page, press Sign in on trialexit.vercel.app. Sign in with Google or email. The extension connects to your account automatically and your trials sync with the web app.

    Prefer not to make an account? Choose “Continue without an account”. Everything still works on this computer.

That's it. Here's what happens now

  • Start a free trial anywhere and TrialExit asks: “Trial started — track it?” with the end date and price filled in.
  • You get a nudge 2 days, 1 day, 6 hours and 1 hour before the charge, with a button that opens the exact cancel page.
  • Subscriptions you track get a reminder 3 days and 1 day before every renewal.
  • Optional: in Settings → Trial sources, connect Gmail to find trials, subscriptions, cancellations and offers, including ones from your phone.

Questions

Chrome shows “Disable developer mode extensions”. Is that a problem?

No. Chrome shows this for any extension installed outside the store. Click the × or Keep to dismiss it. It goes away for good once you switch to the store version.

It says “Manifest file is missing or unreadable”

You picked the wrong folder. Choose the folder that has manifest.json directly inside it. On Windows, Extract All sometimes creates a folder inside another folder with the same name; pick the inner one.

How do I update to a newer version?
  1. Download the zip again from this page and unzip it.
  2. Replace the contents of your old trialexit-extension folder with the new files.
  3. Open chrome://extensions and click the circular ↻ arrow on the TrialExit card.

Your trials and settings are kept.

Is it safe?

The extension is built from the open-source code on GitHub. It never asks for bank or card details. Pages you visit are checked on your computer for trial wording and are never uploaded. Read the privacy policy.

How do I remove it?

Open chrome://extensions and click Remove on TrialExit. If you signed in, your trials stay in your account and in the web app.

Can I use it on my phone?

Chrome on phones doesn't support extensions. On your phone, use the web app: it emails you before each trial charges and can scan your Gmail.
